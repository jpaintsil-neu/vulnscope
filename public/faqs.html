Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about VulnScope, its public data, and your workspace reviews.

Getting Started

What is VulnScope?

VulnScope is designed to make cybersecurity vulnerability information easier to understand and organize. It brings together vulnerability records, product information, educational resources, and personal review workflows.

Who is VulnScope for?

VulnScope supports everyday technology users, cybersecurity students, and security professionals. Start with Resources for foundational concepts or explore the workspaces for record details.

Do I need an account to read these pages?

No. You can read the Home, About, Resources, and FAQs pages without signing in. Account registration is not currently available.

Understanding the Data

Where does the public data come from?

VulnScope is designed around public records from the National Vulnerability Database (NVD), maintained by NIST. Vulnerability information comes from CVE records, while product identifiers come from the CPE Dictionary.

What is a CVE identifier?

A Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) identifier provides a shared name for a publicly disclosed vulnerability. Its associated record provides a description and references for further review.

Does a severity score mean my technology is affected?

No. CVSS describes vulnerability severity. A score alone does not establish whether your particular product or version is affected. Review the affected versions and relevant vendor references.

Workspace Reviews

Do review notes change the original NVD record?

No. Review notes and locally managed records are separate from public source information. Changes within VulnScope do not modify records published by NVD.

How do vulnerability and product reviews differ?

Vulnerability reviews focus on security issues, triage statuses, and analyst notes. Product reviews focus on technology identifiers, product details, review statuses, and notes. Each workspace has its own review workflow.

What should I check when a search has no results?

Check the spelling of the identifier, vendor, or product name. Try broader keywords and remove restrictive filters. An empty result does not establish that a product has no vulnerabilities.
