Frequently Asked Questions

Answers about VulnScope, its public data, and your workspace reviews.

Getting Started

What is VulnScope?

VulnScope is designed to make cybersecurity vulnerability information easier to understand and organize. It brings together vulnerability records, product information, educational resources, and personal review workflows.

Who is VulnScope for?

VulnScope supports everyday technology users, cybersecurity students, and security professionals. Start with Resources for foundational concepts or explore the workspaces for record details.

Do I need an account to read these pages?

No. You can read the Home, About, Resources, and FAQs pages without signing in. Account registration is not currently available.

Understanding the Data

Where does the public data come from?

The current vulnerability workspace uses an imported sample of CVE records from the National Vulnerability Database (NVD), maintained by NIST.

What is a CVE identifier?

A Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE) identifier provides a shared name for a publicly disclosed vulnerability. Its associated record provides a description and references for further review.

Does a severity score mean my technology is affected?

No. CVSS describes vulnerability severity. A score alone does not establish whether your particular product or version is affected. Review the affected versions and relevant vendor references.

Workspace Reviews

Do review notes change the original NVD record?

No. Review notes and locally managed records are separate from public source information. Changes within VulnScope do not modify records published by NVD.

How do vulnerability and product reviews differ?

Vulnerability reviews focus on security issues, triage statuses, and analyst notes. Product reviews focus on technology identifiers, product details, review statuses, and notes in their own workspace.

What should I check when a search has no results?

Check the spelling of the identifier, vendor, or product name. Try broader keywords and remove restrictive filters. An empty result does not establish that a product has no vulnerabilities.

Products Workspace

Where does the product data come from?

Public products are an imported sample of the NVD Common Platform Enumeration (CPE) Dictionary, maintained by NIST. The data reflects the last successful import.

NVD Product Documentation
What is a CPE name?

A CPE name is a standardized identifier for a specific technology. It records the product type, vendor, product, and version. VulnScope shows these as separate columns and keeps the full CPE name on each product's details page.

Why does the same product appear more than once?

The CPE Dictionary has a separate entry for each version of a product, so each version appears as its own record.

What does "Not specified" mean for a version?

This means that the CPE entry doesn't name a specific version. It either covers any version or a version does not apply.

Does a listed product have known vulnerabilities?

Not necessarily. A product record only identifies the technology. Search the Vulnerabilities workspace and check vendor advisories to see if a product or version is affected.

What does a local product record save?

The local product records store the product name, vendor, optional version, product type, review status, exposure notes, and comments. They also have the creation/update dates and your workspace identifier.

How do I add, edit, or delete a product record?

Click on 'Add Product' on the Products page. To edit or delete, choose 'My local records', open the record, and select 'Edit record' or 'Delete record'. All the public product records are read-only.

Workspace Data and Privacy

What local information is saved?

Local vulnerability records store your title, description, optional CVE identifier, severity, review status, and any notes, explanations, or protective guidance you enter. The database also stores creation/update dates and a workspace identifier used to associate the records with your browser workspace.

How does the workspace cookie work?

Creating a local record establishes a random workspace cookie in your browser. The server uses it to identify your local workspace. The cookie has a lifetime of up to one year, renewed when you create another local record. Browsing public CVEs does not require a workspace cookie.

Who can access my local workspace?

Local-record pages and actions are scoped to the workspace cookie presented by your browser. Another browser or private browsing session normally has a separate workspace. This guest feature does not provide account login or cross-device access. Database administrators can access stored records.

Does clearing cookies delete my records?

No. Clearing or losing the workspace cookie removes that browser's access to its existing local records; the records remain in the database. Cookie expiration also does not automatically delete records. Delete records you no longer want while you still have access to the workspace.

How can I delete a local record?

Open Vulnerabilities, choose My local records, and open the record. Select Delete record and confirm the deletion. This removes the record from the application's database. Imported public CVEs are read-only and cannot be deleted through local-record controls.

Which external services does the site use?

The current pages load the Manrope font from Google Fonts, making requests to Google's font servers. Public vulnerability data is imported from NVD. Links to CVE, FIRST, NVD, and GitHub take you to services with their own privacy practices. Feedback submitted through this project's GitHub Issues is public.
